Privacy Policy — Zenith UI

Last updated: 11 November 2026

Zenith UI is a home screen replacement (launcher) for Android. This policy explains what the app does with your information. The short version: Zenith works without an account, and without one nothing you do in it leaves your phone — with two exceptions you choose: the Weather widget, which sends the place you pick to a weather service, and opening a Look somebody shared, which asks Zenith's server for it. If you choose to make a Zenith account, the app sends Zenith's server only what is listed under "A Zenith account" below.

What Zenith UI collects

Without an account: nothing. Zenith UI has no analytics, no advertising and no crash reporting, and no personal data is sold or shared. Until you make a Zenith account or sign in to one, the only network traffic the app produces is Google Play Billing checking with Google Play whether you own Zenith Plus — see Payments below — and, only if you ask for them, the requests described under "The Weather widget" and "Opening a Look".

The Weather widget

The Weather widget is off until you choose a place for it. When you search for one, what you type is sent to Open-Meteo's place search (geocoding-api.open-meteo.com). Once you choose a place, its latitude and longitude — and nothing else: no name, no account, no identifier — are sent to Open-Meteo's forecast service (api.open-meteo.com), at most every half hour while the home screen is showing, and the forecast that comes back is kept on your phone. Zenith does not use your phone's location for this; it only ever sends the place you picked. Like any server, Open-Meteo sees the internet address the request comes from; its own terms and privacy policy, at open-meteo.com, cover what it does with that. Choosing "Stop getting the weather" in the widget's sheet stops the requests.

The island

The island (Zenith Plus) is black round your phone's camera, shown over other apps. It needs Android's "Display over other apps" permission, which you give in Android's settings and can take back there at any time. It shows the music playing, a call or timer that is running, the ringer when you change it, the battery as you plug in, and, for a moment, a notification as it arrives: whose it is, and its message if you tap the island to read it. It reads these through the notification access described under Permissions, if you have given it. Nothing it shows is kept, logged or sent anywhere.

Opening a Look

A Look is a home screen design: its colours, wallpaper design, icon choices and layout. Under Settings > Looks you can type a code somebody gave you, or tap Featured looks. Either one asks Zenith's server for the Look, with or without an account: the request holds the code, or asks for the list, and nothing about you. Like any server, Zenith's sees the internet address the request comes from, as described under "A Zenith account". The Look that comes back is put on your home screen and kept on your phone only if you save it. Nothing is sent until you tap.

A Zenith account

An account is optional. It makes your Aura code count, for the friend who uses it and for you, and with Zenith Plus it can keep a backup of your home screen and share Looks. You make one in the app under Settings > Account, with a username and a password or with Continue with Google. From then on the app talks to Zenith's server, which is run by the developer, over an encrypted (HTTPS) connection, and the server keeps:

  • How you sign in. Either your username and a salted hash of your password — never the password itself — or, with Continue with Google, the number Google uses to identify your Google account to this app. The sign-in token Google hands the app also contains your email address and name; the server reads the token once to check that it is genuine and keeps only that number. Zenith asks for no email address, name or age of its own.
  • Your Aura code, and a record of each time a code is used: whose code it was, who used it and when. That is how the server counts. The person whose code you use sees their count go up; they are not told who you are.
  • One backup of your home screen, only if you have Zenith Plus and tap "Back up to your account". It holds what a backup file holds: your layout (which apps, folders and widgets you placed and where), your preferences, the name you typed during setup, the text of the Quick Note widget, your Aura points and streak, and the list of apps you chose to hide. It never holds the PIN for hidden apps or anything about your purchase. Each new backup replaces the one before. It is kept as it was sent, not locked with a password of its own, so whoever runs the server could read it.
  • The Looks you share, only if you have Zenith Plus and tap Share on a Look. A shared Look holds how your home screen looks — its colours, wallpaper design, icon shape, pack and style, the grid, and which apps, folders and Zenith widgets are where — and the name you gave the Look. It never holds your name, your notes or lists, the apps you hid, photos, other apps' widgets, the names you gave apps, or anything about Aura or your purchase. Anybody who has its code can open it; it is shown to nobody else unless the developer chooses it for the Featured list. An account keeps its twenty newest. Stop sharing, or deleting the Look in the app, removes it from the server.
  • A sign-in key for each phone you are signed in on, stored as a hash.

Like any server, it also sees the internet (IP) address a request comes from. It uses that to limit how often one address can try to sign in, and the address appears in the server's access log beside the time and the kind of request. Passwords, sign-in keys and the contents of requests are never written to a log, and log entries are deleted as the log rotates.

While you are signed in, the app asks the server how your account stands when you open the Account or Backup page and, at most every few minutes, when you return to the home screen; that is how a friend using your code reaches your phone. None of this is used for advertising, sold, or shared with anyone. Google takes part only if you choose Continue with Google, and its own privacy policy covers its side.

Deleting your account

You can delete your account at any time: in the app under Settings > Account > Delete account, or without the app at zenithlabz.duckdns.org/delete-account. Deleting removes the account, its sign-in keys, its Aura code, its backup and the Looks it shared from the server at once. The records of codes used stay, with nothing left in them that says whose they were. Signing out removes the sign-in key for that phone. Uninstalling the app does not delete an account. For a copy of what is kept about your account, write to the address at the end.

What is stored on your device

The app saves the following in its own private storage, readable only by Zenith UI:

  • The name you type during setup, used to greet you on your home screen.
  • Your home screen layout: which apps, folders and widgets you placed and where.
  • Your preferences: light/dark mode, your wallpaper's colours, glass strength, grid size, icon size and shape, how widgets look, and the icon pack you selected.
  • The text of the Quick Note widget, the To-do widget's list, the Countdown's name and date, the World clock's cities and the Weather widget's place, if you use them.
  • Names you gave apps, icons you chose for them, and photos you picked for an app's icon or the Photo frame widget, copied small into the app's own storage.
  • Whether you own Zenith Plus, and the date your free trial began.
  • Colours you built in an earlier version, the apps you chose to hide, and the PIN you set for them, if you use those features. The PIN keeps a list tidy; it is not a device security measure and is stored as you typed it.
  • Your Aura points, your daily streak and the day you last checked in, the themes you unlocked with points, your Aura code, and whether you have already used someone else's. Without a Zenith account none of this is sent anywhere.
  • If you are signed in to a Zenith account: the sign-in key for this phone, what the server last said about the account and, with Google sign-in, the email address of that Google account, so the Account page can show who is signed in. These are left out of Android's own backups and phone-to-phone transfers.

All of this stays on the device, except for what you send to a Zenith account as described above. Uninstalling the app, or clearing its storage in Android Settings, deletes it from the device permanently.

Payments

Zenith Plus is optional and sold through Google Play in three forms: a subscription billed every month, the same subscription billed every three months, and a one-time lifetime pass. Google handles the payment and tells the app one thing: whether this Google account currently owns Plus. Zenith UI never sees your card, your name or your address, and stores nothing about the payment beyond that yes or no. Subscriptions are managed and cancelled in the Google Play app. Google's own privacy policy covers what Play does with the transaction.

The seven-day free trial is counted on your phone from the moment you accept it, unless Google Play offers it as part of the subscription, in which case Google counts it.

A backup file you export contains your layout and settings, and is written only where you choose to put it. Restoring a backup never changes your purchase or your trial.

Zenith's server is told nothing about your purchase. Backing up to your account is a Zenith Plus feature, but whether you own Plus is checked on your phone.

What Zenith UI reads from your device

To work as a launcher, the app reads:

  • The list of installed applications, through Android's LauncherApps service, so it can show your apps and their icons. The list as a whole is never sent anywhere. A backup you send to a Zenith account names the apps you placed on your home screen and the ones you hid, as described above.
  • Your wallpaper, once you have made Zenith UI your default home app, so glass panels can show a blurred copy of it. The image is used only for drawing and is never stored or transmitted.
  • Battery level and charging state, for the Power widget, via a standard system broadcast.
  • Today's calendar entries, but only if you grant calendar access, from the Calendar widget's "Show your events" or the bedside clock's "Allow calendar". The titles, times and calendar colours are read each time they are shown, displayed on your device, and never stored by Zenith UI or sent anywhere. Declining leaves every other feature working.
  • The motion sensor (accelerometer), only while the home screen is showing, so the light on the glass panels moves, and the icons float, as you tilt the phone. Readings are used for drawing and are never stored or sent anywhere.
  • Notifications, only if you give Zenith notification access in Android's settings, for notification dots and the Now playing widget. Zenith counts how many notifications each app has showing, and reads the title, artist and album picture of whatever is playing; it does not read, keep or send what your notifications say.
  • Apps' shortcuts and widgets, through Android's launcher services, so they can be shown and started from the home screen. They are drawn on the phone and never sent anywhere.
  • Nothing through the lock helper. If you choose a gesture that locks the phone, Android asks you to switch on Zenith's lock helper, an accessibility service. It asks Android for no events and cannot see the screen; its only use is pressing lock when you make the gesture.

Zenith UI does not request location, contacts, storage, camera or microphone permissions. Calendar access is optional, requested only by the Calendar widget and the bedside clock, and can be revoked at any time in Android Settings without affecting the rest of the launcher. The torch tile uses the system camera flash API, which needs no permission.

Leaving the app

Searching the web from Search or the app drawer hands your query to the search app or browser you already have installed. What happens after that is covered by that app's own privacy policy, not this one. The same applies to any app you launch from Zenith UI.

Children

Zenith UI is suitable for all ages and is not aimed at children. Without an account it collects no data from anyone, including children. An account is optional and asks for no name, age or email address, and it can be deleted at any time as described above.
